# Subscribed licences

The **Subscribed licences** page shows what each licence contract covers, read the way the licence position reads it: the **bundles** of the contract (one seat for all their modules), its **lines of one module grouped by how they are counted**, the **figures the customer declared** for it, and, on the side, **what each way of counting means**. It is the *what we are entitled to* half of the licence equation, for every contract of any connected application; the other half, what is used and what it may cost, is on **Reviews → Licences**.

The page only reads. A contract, its lines, its bundles and its declared figures are entered on the [CSI](./csi.md) screen: **Change the contracts** opens it.

Open it from **Licences → Subscribed licences**.

## One block per contract \{#contract\}

Each contract is a block, in the order of its identifier. Its head gives the description and the contract number, a **not active** badge when the contract is not in force, its validity (*from → to*) and the applications it **covers** — those linked to it on the *Applications* tab of the contract (*no application yet* otherwise). A contract that covers no application counts for none of them.

Inside the block, three parts, each shown only when it holds something:

| Part | What it lists |
|---|---|
| **Bundles** | One card per bundle of the contract: its name and code, its quantity and metric, and the modules it groups (*no module in it yet* when it is empty). One seat counts for every module of the bundle. |
| **Lines of one module** | The contract's lines on a single module, one table per way of counting: *Module*, *Metric*, *Subscribed*. |
| **Declared by the customer** | The figures declared for the contract, dated: *On*, *Revenue (millions)*, *Employees*, *Comment*. *Nothing declared yet.* when the list is empty. |

The module names are shortened: the *JD Edwards EnterpriseOne* prefix is left out.

## How a line is counted \{#how-counted\}

A line is grouped by its metric. The side panel says the same thing on the page, with the sign-in period of the application and the core factor in force.

| Group | Metrics | What it is compared with |
|---|---|---|
| **Counted in users** | Application User, Named User Plus on an application module | Each enabled user is one seat, on the modules they used — or, without usage tracking, on those their security allows when they signed in within the application's period (90 days by default). A bundle is tried before the module alone, the smaller one that fits first. |
| **Counted in named users** | Named User Plus on a database, an option or a pack | Not seat by seat — JD Edwards signs in to the database with one account — but a count: the enabled JD Edwards users of the contract's applications, the open accounts of the database that are not Oracle's own, and the users declared for the other applications; never below the minimum per processor of the price list. An Enterprise option or pack follows its database. |
| **Counted on the revenue** | Enterprise $M | The line covers its modules for everybody; its quantity is a revenue in millions, compared with the revenue declared for the contract. |
| **Counted on the employees** | Employee | The line covers its modules for everybody; it is compared with the employees declared, else those collected from the application. |
| **Counted on the processors** | Processor | Compared with the cores of the database servers × the core factor of their chip (0.5 for Intel and AMD), a virtual machine counting every physical host of its cluster. The sizes are those stated in nomajde's [Server sizes](../../nomajde/monitoring/server-sizes.md#processor-licences), else the CPU count collected. |
| **Not compared** | Concurrent User, Expense Report, Stream… | The line covers its modules; nothing is compared. |

:::info[JDE-specific]
The usage behind *Counted in users* is Object Usage Tracking, and the employees collected come from JD Edwards EnterpriseOne. For another source, the lines are read the same way; the usage comes from what its connector collects.
:::

## Tips & best practices

- **Check the groups before reading the position.** A line in the wrong group is almost always a wrong metric on the contract line — fix it on the [CSI](./csi.md) screen.
- **Declare the revenue and the employees** of a contract that holds an *Enterprise $M* or an *Employee* line: without a declared figure, nothing can be compared.
- **Bundles first.** When the contract sells modules as a suite, enter the bundle with its modules: a user of two of them then takes one seat, not two.
- **Ask the assistant.** It is given the number of contracts and bundles shown on the page.
