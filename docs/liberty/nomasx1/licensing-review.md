# Licensing review

**Licences**, in the **Reviews** folder of the Nomasx-1 menu, is the licence position of a connected application: the lines of its Oracle contracts and who is counted on each one, the lines counted another way (revenue, employees, processors, Named User Plus), what is missing, the modules no line covers, and what all this could cost. The page is titled **Licensing review**; one application at a time, two tabs — **Usage** and **Financial risk** — and a PDF of the same reading.

It is written for the licence manager and whoever prepares a renewal: the aim is internal compliance — what the company needs against what it subscribed — before any discussion with the vendor.

:::warning[An assumption, never a certified position]
The page says it at the top, the PDF on its cover and under the estimate: the reading applies an understanding of the contract to what was collected, and the amounts are **public list prices with three years of support**. Only Oracle can state a licence position. Use the page to understand the position and to prepare the discussion.
:::

:::info[JDE-specific]
The licensing review reads JD Edwards EnterpriseOne applications: their users, their security and their Object Usage Tracking, as the licence collection gathers them. Another connected application has no licence position; its users can still be declared on a contract when a database is licensed per Named User Plus (see [Users of other applications](./licenses/csi.md#tab-other-users)).
:::

## The header \{#header\}

| Element | What it does |
|---|---|
| **Application** | The JD Edwards application to read, picked by number and name. The page remembers the last one picked. |
| **Get the report (PDF)** | Writes the same reading as a document, in the language of the page — see [The PDF report](#report). |
| **Read again** | Reads the position anew: the page always reads what is in the database at the moment it opens. |

Under the warning, five figures:

| Figure | Means |
|---|---|
| **Users counted** | The people whose needs were read (see [Who is counted](./how-a-contract-is-counted.md#who)). |
| **On a licence** | Those who found a seat on at least one line. |
| **Missing seats** | Users who need a licensed module and found no seat left on any line that covers it — one per user and module. |
| **On a module not covered** | Users of a module that no line of the contracts covers. |
| **Estimated risk** | The total of the *Financial risk* tab, in euros. |

The line under the figures says **which basis was applied**: the modules people *used* — when Object Usage Tracking has data for the application — or the modules their security *allows*, for those who signed in within the application's period (90 days unless set otherwise on [Applications](./settings/global/applications.md)). It also gives the number of enabled users, technical and linked accounts left out.

A contract is attached to an application on its **Applications** tab ([CSI](./licenses/csi.md)). Until one is, the page says *No contract covers this application yet*.

## Usage tab \{#usage\}

The lines of the contracts and who is on them, in four or five blocks.

### Lines counted in users \{#lines-users\}

One row per line counted in people (*Application User*, *Named User Plus* on an application module): the module, or the bundle with its components; the contract (CSI); **Subscribed**, **Used** and **Free**, and a bar that turns orange when the line is full. A line marked **bundle** counts a user once for all its modules. A line on a component that includes others says so (*includes Core Tools and Infrastructure, Oracle Standard Edition 2* on Oracle Technology Foundation).

### Lines counted another way \{#lines-other\}

The lines that are not seats, one figure per contract and metric:

| Metric | Compared with | What the last column says |
|---|---|---|
| **Revenue (millions)** | The revenue declared on the contract (*Declared figures* tab of the CSI). | *declared on* and the date. |
| **Employees** | The employees declared on the contract, else those collected from JD Edwards. | *declared on…* or *collected from JD Edwards*. |
| **Processors** | The processor licences the database servers need. | The cores × the core factor, the servers, a cluster counted whole, and where the sizes come from. |
| **Named User Plus** | A database, its options and its packs: a count, never below the minimum per processor. | The JD Edwards users + the database accounts + the users declared, and the minimum. |

**Beyond** is what exceeds the subscription; *within* when nothing does; *not declared* when no figure is known. The rules are on [How a contract is counted](./how-a-contract-is-counted.md). A line whose metric the reading does not count (concurrent user, expense report, stream…) is listed under the table: it covers its modules and nothing is compared.

### Missing seats and modules no line covers \{#gaps\}

- **Missing seats** — per module, the users who need it and found no seat left; the first eight are named, **+N more** shows the others.
- **Modules no line covers** — per module, the users of a module that is in no contract. An audit would count one *Application User* each: a risk to negotiate (a line to add, a bundle to extend), not a fact.

### Other Oracle products of the contracts \{#products\}

When the application's contracts describe an installation of **Oracle Data Integrator** or **Oracle Analytics Server**, each one appears with its contract, an **Open** button to its page, and its findings, each with its level: **risk**, **to check**, **not known** or **fine**. These products are described by questionnaire on their own pages — [Oracle Data Integrator](./licenses/odi.md) and [Oracle Analytics Server](./licenses/analytics-server.md) — and the findings read the same there, here and in the PDF.

What the gaps of the *Usage* tab could cost, per block, each with its total:

| Block | Priced as |
|---|---|
| **Missing seats** | Users × (list price + 3 × yearly support) of the module's *Application User* price. |
| **Modules no line covers** | The same, one *Application User* per user. |
| **Named User Plus beyond the subscription** | What exceeds the subscription × (Named User Plus list price + 3 × yearly support) of the database, option or pack. |
| **What these products are short of** | The findings of Data Integrator and Analytics Server that carry an amount. |

Each row gives **List price**, **Support / year**, **Each (3 years of support)** and **Estimate**; a module with no price in [Pricing](./settings/pricing/pricing.md) says *no price* and adds nothing. The prices are those of [Settings → Licenses → Pricing → Pricing](./settings/pricing/pricing.md).

The revenue, employee and processor lines are not priced: when one of them is beyond its subscription, a banner at the bottom says how many and sends back to *Usage*.

## The PDF report \{#report\}

**Get the report (PDF)** writes the licensing review of the application as a document, in the language the page is shown in: a cover that says *Assumption — not a certified position*, the five figures and the basis applied, the lines counted in users, the lines counted another way, the other Oracle products, the estimated financial risk block by block, the estimate by module, and who is concerned. The amounts are those of the page.

It is not the [licence audit report](./reports/audit-licences.md), the full document for the steering committee — which reads the same position for its financial section.

---

## Access \{#access\}

The four predefined roles of Nomasx-1 — auditor, security officer, licence manager, administrator — open the page and its PDF. A role built by hand needs the **Reviews → Licences** entry and the right to read the licence position (`plugin:nomasx1.licence_position`), which the predefined roles carry. The AI assistant of Nomasx-1 reads the same position when asked about licences, and says the same thing: an assumption at list prices (see [AI assistant](./ai-assistant.md)).

## Tips & best practices

- **Collect before you read.** The page reads what the licence collection gathered: run **Jobs → Collect licences** first (see [Jobs](./jobs.md)).
- **Fill the contract as it is written.** Bundles, their components, the declared revenue and employees: without them, a bundle's users fall on the module's own line or become missing seats, and a revenue line says *not declared*.
- **State the server sizes in Nomajde.** Processor lines and the Named User Plus minimum follow the sizes of [Server sizes](../nomajde/monitoring/server-sizes.md); without them the reading falls back to the CPU count the database gave, read as x86.
- **Read *Modules no line covers* first.** It is usually the largest amount and the easiest to discuss: a module used by a handful of people can be taken away from their security, or added to a bundle.
- **Keep the subscribed side current.** The contracts themselves are read on [Subscribed licences](./licenses/subscribed-licences.md).
